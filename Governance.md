# Governance

FLOW is an openly implementable standard stewarded by its founder-led project,
not an independently governed foundation standard.

The project steward controls the official FLOW name, repositories, releases,
and conformance marks and has final editorial authority. Significant technical
changes, specification proposals, and feedback are discussed in public
repository discussions (https://github.com/jiggy/jig/discussions), with the
reason for the final decision recorded publicly. Anyone may propose changes or
report implementation experience.

Published major versions of an Approved Specification are stable. During the
initial public alpha and while specifications carry candidate identifiers (such
as `/0`), drafts and prerelease SDKs remain provisional: draft semantics may be
revised in place to incorporate community feedback before finalization, without
creating prerelease compatibility obligations. An Approved Specification is not
silently redefined; breaking changes to an approved specification require a new
major version. Anyone may implement FLOW, publish an independent implementation
or catalogue, and use the open conformance material without paying the steward.
Optional paid verification or support does not change those rights.

Sponsorship may fund maintenance, documentation, SDKs, conformance, and
ecosystem work. It does not purchase a specification change, veto, private
draft access, or technical authority.

The steward may appoint maintainers or an advisory group and may later broaden
governance when independent adoption warrants it. Until such a change is
published here, final editorial authority remains with the project steward.

## Specification status

All specification work is a Draft Specification under the Community
Specification License 1.0 until the steward identifies an exact version and
commit as an Approved Specification in a public release record. Prerelease SDK
publication, registry alpha tags, or candidate test suites do not constitute
an Approved Specification. Once approved, a version can be revised only through
a new, separately identified version.
